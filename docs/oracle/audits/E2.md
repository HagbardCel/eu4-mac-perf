- Owns `tests/test_oracle_certify_adversarial_acceptance.py` and
  `tests/oracle_certify_acceptance/`.

- Does **not** certify a real EU IV session.

## Merge discipline

1. Author tests on `feature/oracle-e2-audit` from amendment `8632d66`.
2. Evaluate against E1 in a **disposable** worktree (E2 tests + E1 code). Do not
   copy acceptance tests into the E1 branch.
3. Keep the E2 PR **unmerged** until `certify_session` exists on its target base.
4. After E1 merges, run normal CI on E2 and merge independently.

## Candidate binding (acceptance contract)

E2 prepares known-good candidate **inputs** (`acceptance_candidate.py`: frozen P4
fields and a real RE1 executable copy). That fixture does not establish EU IV runtime
provenance and is not authoritative trusted-build metadata.

E1 must independently verify submitted source and executable identities and establish
any source-to-build relationship it claims (controlled build or E1-owned records per
P4). Caller-supplied digests and acceptance-generated records do not prove provenance.

Integration tests require a **binding-specific** failure in `promotion.reasons` or
`evaluation.failures` when the submitted executable digest disagrees with the artifact
on disk, with eligible reference admission.

## Negative controls

- `false_certifier_stand_in`: schema-valid `certified` with a recorded mismatch.
- `forged_executable_binding_stand_in`: inconsistent executable binding (helper-level).
- `unrelated_failure_stand_in`: eligible admission, failed promotion without binding
  diagnostics (must not satisfy the binding-failure helper).

Each control is rejected inside the test module; CI stays green.

## Audit verdict

Blocked until E1’s implementation and evidence are reviewed on a tree that
includes both packages with **zero skips** in `CertifySessionImplementationTests`.
Passing synthetic adversarial tests on an E2-only tree does not establish
live-grounded Tier 1 certification.
